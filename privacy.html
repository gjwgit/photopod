PhotoPod Privacy Policy

Effective 17 November 2026 · Version 1.3.3 · Togaware Pty Ltd

PhotoPod does not collect your data. Your photos and videos are kept in your own Solid Pod, encrypted, not on our servers. There is no account with us, no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising and no tracking of any kind within the app. The only time any photo reaches a service we run is when you ask for faces or tags to be found in it, as described below.

Your photos, encrypted in your own Pod

PhotoPod stores everything under photopod/data/ in the Solid Pod you log in to, on a Solid server you choose. Each photo and video is encrypted on your device, with your security key, before it is sent. So are the smaller copies PhotoPod keeps of each photo for the grid and the preview, your albums, your favourites, the people and tags found in your photos, and the list of what you have shared.

A photo's own name is kept inside its encryption, and the file is stored under a random name that keeps only its extension, such as .jpg. The Solid server can see how many files there are, whether each is a photo or a video, how large they are and when they changed, but not what they show, what they are called or where they were taken. Folders and albums have random names too. PhotoPod never sends your security key anywhere.

Sharing

When you share a photo, a folder or an album, it is with the people you name, by their WebID, using Solid's own access control. They are given access and the key to read what you shared, and nothing else. You can take the access away again.

Identify People and Tag Photos

The People and Tags sections are filled in by the PhotoPod Analyser, a service the PhotoPod team runs on the Australian Solid Community's server. It looks only at the photos you select, and only when you tap Identify People or Tag Photos:

  • PhotoPod puts a preview-sized copy of each selected photo into a folder of your Pod, encrypted with a key of its own, and shares just that folder with the Analyser. The copies have their details removed first: where and when the photo was taken, the camera, and any captions.
  • The Analyser decrypts the copies only in its memory. They are never written to its disk and never logged; its log records only how many photos each request held.
  • It writes what it found back into the same folder, encrypted, and PhotoPod files it with your other encrypted data. Once every photo is answered, usually within seconds, PhotoPod withdraws the share and removes the folder. The Analyser keeps nothing about you.
  • To recognise the same person again, what is found for each face includes a numerical description of it. That is kept only in your Pod, encrypted, with the names you give.

The Analyser's source is part of PhotoPod, in analyser/face_analyser. If you never tap those buttons, no photo is ever shared with it.

On your device

  • How you like to view your album, such as the layout, the order and the map style, is kept on the device and never sent anywhere.
  • The pictures PhotoPod shows are decrypted in memory and dropped when the app closes. Videos play from memory too.
  • If you log in, the login is kept on the device so you stay logged in.
  • Download saves a decrypted copy of a photo or video where you choose. That copy is yours to look after and is no longer protected by PhotoPod.

Maps

The Maps section draws its map from a map service: OpenStreetMap unless you choose another, such as CARTO, Esri, OpenTopoMap or CyclOSM, in the map settings. As with any online map, that service sees your network address and which part of the world is being shown, which is where your photos were taken. It is not sent your photos, their names or anything else about them. What a map service does with its requests is covered by its own privacy policy.

Places

Sorting by location names the city or region each photo was taken in from a list bundled with the app, from GeoNames, so where your photos were taken is never sent anywhere to name it.

If you type a place into a photo's Get Info and look it up, that text, and the app's name, is sent to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim to find it, and nothing else. Coordinates you type are used as they are, without asking. That code is lib/services/geocoder.dart.

Checking for a new version

When it starts, PhotoPod fetches its public change log, CHANGELOG.md, from GitHub, to show its version and whether a newer one is out. It is a plain download of a public file, and PhotoPod sends nothing with it.

Before you log in, and on the web

Before you log in, PhotoPod shows a sample album that is bundled with the app. Nothing of yours is involved.

PhotoPod Web is the same app served to your browser. The web server delivers the app's files, and like any web server sees the requests for them. The app itself behaves exactly as described here.

Children

PhotoPod is suitable for all ages and collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version will be published at this address with a new effective date. PhotoPod is free software and its full history is public, so any change to what the app does is visible in the repository.
